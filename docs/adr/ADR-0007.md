# ADR-0007 — Hash-chain audit log entries

## Context

Append-only is necessary but not sufficient for tamper-evidence: an attacker with database access could append a fraudulent entry or modify past entries directly in storage. A hash chain makes any modification of past entries detectable on read.

## Decision

Each audit entry stores its content hash and the hash of the previous entry: `hash_n = SHA256(hash_n-1 || event_n)`. A daily verifier scans the chain and alerts on any mismatch. The chain head is published externally (e.g., to a write-once store or a transparency log) for additional assurance.

## Alternatives

Alternatives considered: (a) signed entries only (no chain) — detects modification of individual entries but not insertion or deletion; (b) Merkle tree — efficient batch verification but complex to maintain incrementally; (c) blockchain — over-engineered. (a) rejected for insertion/deletion blindness; (b) rejected for incremental complexity; (c) rejected for operational cost.

## Consequences

Append is now sequential: entry N cannot be written until entry N-1's hash is known. This bounds append throughput to the single-threaded hash computation rate (~50k/s on modern hardware, well above the 5k/s NFR target). Verification is O(N); daily verifier scans must be partitioned for very large logs.

## Validation Plan

Phase 1: implemented as a stub contract. Phase 8: red-team test that modifies a past entry's payload and verifies the daily verifier detects it. Phase 9: publish chain head to S3 Object Lock and verify external detection. Phase 10: load-test concurrent appends; verify hash chain integrity under contention.
